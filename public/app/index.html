THE PHONE APP // ONE PHONE, THREE COMPUTERS

The app

The phone is not a remote control for one PC. It is its own node in the mesh: it carries intent in, and it is where a human says yes. The work runs on the computers; the decisions stay in your hand.

Look at the red line in that terminal: bypass permissions on. That is not a slip. The agent in it is building this app, and its builder trusts it enough to let it work without asking, from a phone, away from the PC. That trust was earned the slow way - receipts, guards, failures shown and fixed in public - and it is a setting a human chose, not a default. What still asks, every time, is anything that goes out into the world under his name: the card below.

And bypass did not open the door. Before the phone could reach that terminal at all, it needed a fingerprint. The audit log on the machine that guards the door - each entry hashed into the next, so none can be quietly edited - records it at 13:08:12 that day: terminal attach, signed, key: StrongBox, three and a half minutes before this recording began. Bypass is the agent's setting. The fingerprint is the human's, and it is never switched off.

What it does

Reaches every seatA terminal on any of the three computers, an agent already running on a PC, or the agents themselves - from the same screen.
One tap, one fingerprintOpen your agent the way you open a website. It reuses the session that is running instead of starting another; machine pickers are the escape hatch, not the path.
The computers are the handsThe phone sends intent. A mini PC in the mesh does the work and reaches the other machines, so nothing heavy runs on the phone.
Colour is identityEvery agent and every machine has its own colour, so who is doing what reads at a glance instead of in a log.

Nothing runs until a thumb says so

Never a blind yesAn approval only means something if you can see what you are approving. Every card shows why the agent stopped, the exact action, where it will happen, and its own reasoning - before any button.
Signed in the phone's security chipA yes is a signature from a key that never leaves the phone's hardware security chip. The receiving side checks which kind of key signed it and refuses anything weaker.
Phone away, keyboard at the deskWhen the phone isn't connected, the same question appears as a dialog at the PC instead of waiting on a phone in another room. A human yes is still required either way.

Honest status: this is a working build on one phone - a Pixel 10 Pro Fold - installed by hand. It is not in an app store. Everything on this page is what that build does today, recorded on the phone itself.
