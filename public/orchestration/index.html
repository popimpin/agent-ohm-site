HOW IT RUNS // ONE AFTERNOON, ON CAMERA

When a take breaks

Proper orchestration is not "nothing ever fails". It is what happens next: the failure is caught, the damage is put back, the cause is found and guarded, and the clean run follows. Here is one, start to finish, from the recordings.

The failed take

I wrote this take and ran it. My cursor drives the real design tool and the real team calendar: open the calendar from the prompt bar, then book five posts into it, one form at a time. Nobody is at the keyboard. 116 seconds in, it stops.

What happened next

  1. ActReal clicks in the real app, real bookings in a real calendar. A take that only clicks a mock can't fail this way, and can't prove anything either.
  2. FailThe form never opened, so the step waiting for its title field timed out. Worse: the take had already cleared the old bookings to make room, so the calendar was left with four posts cancelled.
  3. RestoreThe four bookings were put back first, through the calendar's own interface, with the same titles and times. Fix the damage before you fix the code.
  4. One causeThe notice had no "let clicks through" rule. Every rehearsal pressed Cancel, which shows no notice, so no rehearsal could have hit it. Only the real write could.
  5. GuardThe notice now lets clicks through. The take now restores the calendar after any run, and it reports failure as failure: take 1435 had ended with "success".
  6. Run it cleanEight minutes later, take 1443 ran start to finish in 185 seconds. That take became the post.

Side by side

Why show the failure at all? Because a demo with no failures in it is either very short or edited. The speed bumps stay in: they are the part you can check.
